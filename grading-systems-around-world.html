International Grading Systems: A Student Guide

There is no single worldwide grading scale. A 3.5, 8.5 or 85% can mean different things depending on the institution and country. This is why international students should treat simple GPA conversion formulas as estimates rather than official evaluations.

Common systems

Common approaches include 4.0 GPA scales, 5.0 scales, 7.0 scales, 10-point CGPA, 20-point percentage-style scales, 100-point systems, and reverse scales such as Germany's 1.0–5.0 convention. Some systems use degree classifications instead of GPA.

Why exact conversion is difficult

Universities can use different grade boundaries, plus/minus rules, credit weighting and pass requirements. A national label therefore does not guarantee one universal formula.

Best practice for applications

Use an online calculator to understand your result and plan applications. For an official admission decision, follow the destination university's instructions or the credential evaluator it names.
