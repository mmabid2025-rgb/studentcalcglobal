GPA CALCULATION GUIDE

Do Classes with More Credits Affect GPA More?

Yes, when your institution uses a credit-weighted GPA. A course with more credits has more influence on the average than a course with fewer credits. For example, a 4-credit course has four times the weight of a 1-credit course in the calculation.

How credit hours affect GPA

In a credit-weighted system, each course's grade-point value is multiplied by its credit hours. The weighted values are added together and divided by the total credits included in the calculation.

GPA = Σ (course grade points × course credits) ÷ Σ course credits

Worked example

This is an arithmetic illustration using a hypothetical 4.0 grade-point scale. It is not a grading rule for any particular university.

Course A: 3.0 grade points × 4 credits = 12 weighted points

Course B: 4.0 grade points × 1 credit = 4 weighted points

Total: 16 weighted points ÷ 5 credits = 3.20 GPA

The simple average of 3.0 and 4.0 would be 3.50, but that would give both courses equal weight. It is not the correct result for this credit-weighted example.

Why a higher-credit course matters

  • A higher-credit course contributes more to the weighted total.
  • A strong result in a higher-credit course can raise the average more than the same result in a lower-credit course.
  • A low result in a higher-credit course can also lower the average more.

Do all universities calculate GPA this way?

No. Institutions can have different rules for repeated courses, pass/fail courses, transferred credits, labs and courses excluded from GPA calculations. Use the grading and academic regulations published by your own institution when calculating an official result.

Important: Do not convert raw marks or percentages into grade points using an assumed scale. Use the grade-point values and credit rules shown in your official transcript or university policy.
