How to Calculate CGPA: Formula, Credits and Examples

CGPA (cumulative grade point average) summarises academic performance across courses or semesters. In a credit-weighted system, a course with more credits has more influence on the result than a course with fewer credits.

This guide explains the common weighted calculation, shows how to calculate CGPA from semester GPAs, and highlights institutional rules that can change the result.

Ready to calculate?

Use the CGPA calculator for semester results or the GPA calculator for individual courses.

1. The credit-weighted CGPA formula

When your institution weights each course by its credit hours, use:

CGPA = Σ (course grade points × course credits) ÷ Σ course credits

  1. Find each course's grade-point value using your institution's grading table.
  2. Multiply each grade-point value by that course's credit hours.
  3. Add all the weighted grade points.
  4. Add the credit hours included in the calculation.
  5. Divide the weighted grade-point total by the included credit total.

Use grade points, not raw marks or percentages, unless your institution specifically defines another calculation.

3. Calculate CGPA from semester GPAs

If you already know each semester's GPA and the credits included in that semester, use:

CGPA = Σ (semester GPA × semester credits) ÷ Σ semester credits

Suppose Semester 1 has a GPA of 3.20 over 15 credits and Semester 2 has a GPA of 3.60 over 18 credits:

  • Semester 1 contribution: 3.20 × 15 = 48.00
  • Semester 2 contribution: 3.60 × 18 = 64.80
  • Total weighted points: 112.80; total credits: 33

CGPA = 112.80 ÷ 33 = 3.42 (rounded to two decimal places).

Do not simply average semester GPAs when credit totals differ. In this example, the simple average is 3.40, which is not the same as the credit-weighted result.

4. What if your transcript shows percentages or letter grades?

Check whether your transcript or university regulations provide an official grade-point conversion table. A percentage, letter grade and grade-point value are not interchangeable by default. Different institutions can assign different grade points to the same percentage or letter.

If your university publishes an official conversion table, use it before applying the weighted formula. If it does not, do not guess a conversion or assume that a 4.0, 5.0 or 10.0 scale applies. The country grading systems directory distinguishes documented examples from entries where a national university scale has not been verified.

A simple linear conversion between grading scales can be misleading. For admissions, use the target institution's published conversion table or follow the instructions of its designated credential evaluator.

5. Institutional rules that can affect CGPA

  • Repeated courses: a repeated grade may replace an earlier grade, or both attempts may remain in the calculation.
  • Withdrawals and incomplete grades: these may be excluded, included later or treated under a separate rule.
  • Pass/fail courses: some courses carry credits but do not contribute grade points.
  • Transfer credits: transferred courses may count toward degree completion without being included in the institutional GPA.
  • Rounding: decimal precision and the point at which rounding occurs can vary.
Important: An online result is an estimate based on the values and rules entered. For progression, scholarships, graduation or admissions, follow the current academic regulations and transcript method of the relevant institution.

6. Common CGPA calculation mistakes

  • Taking a simple average of semester GPAs when semester credit totals differ.
  • Dividing by the number of courses instead of the total included credits.
  • Mixing percentage marks with grade points in one calculation.
  • Using a maximum GPA scale different from the one on the transcript.
  • Including repeated, withdrawn or pass/fail courses without checking university policy.
  • Rounding intermediate values too early.

Frequently asked questions

Is CGPA just the average of all semester GPAs?

Not necessarily. In a credit-weighted system, multiply each semester GPA by the credits included in that semester, then divide by the total included credits. A simple average is equivalent only when the relevant credit weights are equal.

Can I calculate CGPA from percentage marks?

Only after applying the conversion method specified by your institution. There is no single percentage-to-CGPA formula accepted by every university.

Do credit hours affect CGPA?

Yes, when your institution uses a credit-weighted GPA. A course with more included credits has a greater effect on the weighted average.

Should I report CGPA to two decimal places?

Use the precision requested by your university, employer or admissions office. Two decimal places in this guide are for readability, not a universal reporting requirement.
