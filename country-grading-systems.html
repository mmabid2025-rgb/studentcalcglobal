How to calculate CGPA correctly

When a university reports a GPA for each semester, a common cumulative method is a credit-weighted average. Multiply each semester GPA by that semester’s credit hours, add the products, and divide by the total credit hours. This only gives a meaningful CGPA when all semester GPAs use the same scale and the institution applies this method.

Do not convert a percentage, a 0–20 mark, a 1–10 mark, or a reverse scale into a 4.0 GPA by simply dividing and multiplying. Use the official equivalency table published by the university or evaluating body.

Frequently asked questions

Does every university in a country use the same grading system?

No. Universities, faculties, degree levels, and programmes may use different scales and calculation rules. A country-level example is a reference, not a substitute for the grading key on your transcript.

Can I convert any country's grades directly into CGPA?

No. There is no universally valid country-to-CGPA formula. You need the official grade-point conversion published by your institution or the receiving institution.

What information does the CGPA calculator need?

It needs each semester GPA and the credit hours counted for that semester, all on the same scale. It calculates the credit-weighted average and does not apply university-specific repeat, transfer, or exclusion rules.

Why does some country information say “institution-specific”?

Because no single nationwide university GPA scale has been verified for that entry in this directory. We prefer to say that clearly rather than invent a 4.0, 5.0, or 10.0 maximum.
